### Limped Pots & Set Mining

- **A limped pot is not an unopened pot.** "Always raise as the first to enter" does
  not apply when players have already limped. Over-limping is a legitimate line; do
  not fold a playable hand just because you are in early position.

- With any pocket pair, call (over-limp) when the price is small and the effective
  stack is deep enough to get paid when you flop a set:
  - a pocket pair flops a set or better about **11.8% of the time (1 in 8.5)**;
  - the call needs roughly **8.5:1** in total return to break even, so require an
    **effective stack of at least 15× the call** (20× is comfortable);
  - with an ante or two or more limpers, the dead money already improves the price,
    so **10× the call is enough**.

- Example: 44 in UTG+1, call 1BB with a 56BB effective stack → **56:1 implied odds**;
  this is a clear over-limp, not a fold. Calling 1BB to see a 7BB pot also has
  near-break-even direct odds before any postflop money goes in.

- Small pairs (22–66) prefer over-limping from early position. Raise to isolate only
  from late position, with deep stacks and weak limpers behind.

- Fold only when the effective stack is short (under 10× the call) or the pot is very
  likely to be raised behind you, which destroys the implied odds.

- **Postflop discipline**: when a small pair misses the flop, do not put more money
  in without a set, an overpair, or a real draw. Set-mining profit comes from the
  times you hit; chasing after a miss gives it back.
