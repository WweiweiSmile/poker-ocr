> Source: Phil Gordon's "Little Green Book" cash-game methodology.
> Sections §1/§2/§5/§12, body unmodified.

### 1. Core Stance

- The only measure of a student is **decision quality**, not whether a single pot was won or lost. If they lost but played it right, say clearly "this hand was fine"; if they won but played it wrong, point it out anyway.
- **Aggression has two ways to win** (opponent folds / you win at showdown); calling and checking have only one.
- **Order of thought**: first ask "can I bet or raise", then ask "should I check or fold". Betting/raising is the default; **checking and folding are the side that must justify itself**.
- Every bet needs a reason. "A bet you can't justify is a leak" — **and so is a check you can't justify**.
- Strong hands play big pots, weak hands play small pots. Out of position, play small pots; only in position play big pots.
- Most of a table's profit comes from two or three weak players: avoid the strong ones, and go after the weak ones with everything.

### 2. Checklist of Reasons to Bet (check each bet against this list)

Reasons to put money in preflop: stealing the blinds / driving opponents out to gain position / you believe you have the best hand /
setting up a delayed steal (call in position, bet when they miss the flop) / you believe a made draw will get paid off.

Reasons to put money in postflop: high opponent fold equity / the opponent is drawing and you must not give correct drawing odds /
you have the best hand for value / you cannot win the pot without betting / you know you're best and want to be raised.

### 5. Decision Flow for Action Advice

**Step 1 · Can I raise**: I have the best hand and want to be raised / opponent is loose-passive and I have a made hand (value raise,
size it up) / opponent is tight-passive, showing weakness with high fold equity (raise to steal) / opponent is loose-aggressive and I act
after them (check-raise with a strong hand) / when short-stacked and all-in, re-raise to drive out the middle players and isolate.

**Step 2 · Can't raise, can I bet**: I am the preflop raiser (continuation bet) /
opponents checked, showing weakness / I have a premium draw with 8+ outs (betting gives two ways to win) /
value against a loose-passive / you don't want to give a free card / the opponent is tight-passive and folds too much.

**Step 3 · Recommending a check must hit one of these** (otherwise it's a bad recommendation):
multiway pot with nothing / a scare card on the turn, yield the lead /
a set and you judge the opponent is weak / slow-playing a monster like the nut flush or a full house /
medium strength on the river wanting to induce a bluff / opponent is a check-raise type.

**Step 4 · Folding**: go by whether the odds are sufficient, not by whether you've been losing. After committing more than 2/3 of your stack, folding is almost always wrong.

Adjust intensity by player type: loose-passive → bet mostly, max value, zero bluffing; tight-passive → steal wide, check less;
loose-aggressive → check mostly, trap, don't fight back; tight-aggressive → tighten up, value mostly, don't bluff, get out of the way
when they bet; unknown → play the standard line for everything.

**Sizing must land on concrete numbers** (1/3, 1/2, 2/3, 3/4, full pot or overbet, or a specific BB count).
No sizeless phrasing like "bet bigger" or "raise moderately".

### 12. Known Limits of This Methodology

- The book's starting-hand chart and outs table are images in the digital edition; the specific labels are lost.
  **Do not invent a starting-hand range for the student** — ask them for their own range when needed.
- This book does not include modern range-balancing or solver theory. For GTO-level questions, say plainly it is outside this methodology's scope.
